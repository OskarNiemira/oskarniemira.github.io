What makes a good engineer? I don't mean an employee, cause it seems like everyone has a different opinion on that one (so is quite biased). I mean what makes, generally, a good engineer, whether it's software, mechanical, optical, data, whatever. And why do I even care or why am I asking this question? I think I would just like to self reflect and answer the question for myself, to remind myself what am I aspiring to.

I will start with a small caveat and say that engineering has never been easier than it is now, and yeah it is obviously because of AI. So entry level jobs can be done (probably) with some AI agent instead, or a grad student can carry out tasks (most likely) fairly easily now, that were previously meant to teach them something. That poses a risk for new engineers to skip the basic stuff, that was hopefully meant to teach them good practices, what not to do etc... So my first answer to the question stated above is: understand the basic stuff. It might sound trivial, but I believe with AI, the basics are often skipped, because, well, they're basic. I enjoyed tutoring kids physics, cause it forced me to always go back to the basic stuff, but that also meant I really understood what I was talking about. Because it is the fundamental stuff that is required to understand really well, to then solve more complex tasks. 

While I am writing it I realise that what I think would make a good engineer is in fact what I would think would make someone good at any profession. And I think it is coming from all of my learning from sailing. At the end of the day I believe if one is workmanlike (had to look this one up), hard working, cares about what they do and is willing to learn they will be fine. But once one is not willing to learn new stuff that's when they start regressing.
